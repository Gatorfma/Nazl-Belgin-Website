I do not invent the monsters.

They are already in the room. Blue arrives first, then the teeth, then the apology.

Severity is a kind of tenderness that ran out of time.

A painting is finished when the creature stops asking to be prettier.

Chaos, then care. Never the other way around.

Biography

Nazlı Belgin (b. 1984, Izmir) paints creatures that behave badly and mean well. Working in oil, ink, graphite and pastel, she builds figures out of flat, unembarrassed colour — ultramarine grounds, cadmium bodies, a red that refuses to sit still.

Her work circles three long series: Monsters, Evolution and Stone Hills — inheritance, appetite, and the landscape that outlasts both. Text often surfaces in the paint, written quickly, as if the picture were still arguing with itself.

She lives and works in Istanbul.
