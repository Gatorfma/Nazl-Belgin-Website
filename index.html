Hümeyra

I search for what lies beneath the surface.
I speak of the wound, not of beauty.

Color is my protest; form, my act of defiance.
I hold instinct, not the brush.
I use paint to release the voice that gathers inside me.

Art is not therapy, not decor, and never an investment.
Art is an assault.

It is a silent rebellion against numbness, against the aesthetic lie of capital, against sterilized taste.

I paint not on the white walls of museums, but on the dark subconscious of humanity.
History still writhes beneath my skin — I bring that voice to the surface with paint.

I am raw because I am honest.
I am flawed because I am alive.

Creation is not divine imitation — it is the instinct to survive.
That is why my figures scream, my eyes blaze, my body distorts.

In my art, form is not evolution — it is resistance.
I belong nowhere.

I paint the inner eruption that has burned since the Stone Age.
As I look at the walls of the past, I tear down the walls of the present.

For me, art is not rebirth — it is renewed attack.

Biography

Nazlı Belgin was born in 1985 in Tekirdağ, Turkey, and has been interested in art since childhood.

Her journey began by shaping clay figures at an early age and evolved into her first sculptural experience during high school with the support of her art teacher; the sculpture they created together is still exhibited in the schoolyard.

Despite her passion for art, she pursued a degree in Business Administration at the Faculty of Economics and Administrative Sciences following her family’s guidance and later completed her master’s degree in the same field. After a brief career in the banking sector, she left the corporate world to dedicate herself fully to art.

She founded her own art school and later enrolled in the Faculty of Fine Arts, Department of Painting, graduating at the top of her class.

Based in Ankara, Belgin continues her artistic production under the roof of Historia Art Studio. Her work is inspired by human history, mythology, and the visual potential of scientific thought. She uses color and form as expressive tools, creating a visual language that is both intuitive and reflective. In her recent works, hybrid figures combining human and animal forms appear in ironic and sometimes grotesque scenes.

Nazlı Belgin continues to produce within the field of contemporary art, combining personal experience with historical, scientific, and social layers.
